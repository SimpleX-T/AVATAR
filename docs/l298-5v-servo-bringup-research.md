# L298N at 5 V and servo bring-up: source-backed boundaries

This note responds to the measured input readings and to the report that **both real pumps run when directly supplied with 3.3 V or 5 V**. Those observations are useful: neither pump is completely dead, and both can spin at those bench voltages. They do not establish either pump's **rated** voltage, startup/stall current, delivered flow or pressure, or behavior when driven through an L298N under plumbing load. The precise black-pump listing and the user's L298N PCB/servo model remain unidentified; see [the item-evidence note](temu-pump-driver-research.md).

## What “5 V on the L298N” actually means

- The **L298 chip** has two distinct positive supplies: `VS` (power output stages, pin 4) and `VSS` (logic, pin 9). `VSS` must be 4.5–7 V; `VS` must satisfy the datasheet's operative lower limit (`VIH + 2.5 V`). Both motor bridges share **one `VS` rail**. `IN1`–`IN4` and `ENA`/`ENB` accept a logic high from 2.3 V, so a nominal 3.3 V ESP32 control signal can meet the chip's input-high threshold when the supplies and grounds are correct. [ST L298 datasheet, pin functions and electrical characteristics](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/l298.pdf).
- A board terminal or jumper labeled “5V” may refer to the **logic regulator/jumper**, not `VS` or the motor outputs. The chip datasheet does **not** specify how the user's particular L298N module wires its jumper, 5 V terminal, regulator, or protection diodes. Therefore, do not prescribe a jumper position or inject an external 5 V supply into that terminal until the actual board's front/back markings or circuit are identified. Measure its `VS` and logic supply with respect to board GND before attaching motors. [ST chip supply definitions](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/l298.pdf).

The user clarified that the jumper they installed is marked **`5V-EN`**. On
common L298N modules this enables the onboard **logic-supply regulator**; it
does not select a 5 V motor output. This module-level interpretation is still
conditional on inspecting the actual board. The decisive measurement is the
voltage at its motor-supply/`VS` terminal relative to `GND`, with the pumps
disconnected. If that terminal is fed 12 V, the pumps are not receiving a
5 V motor rail merely because `5V-EN` is installed.
- Even when `VS` itself is regulated to 5 V, **the output is not 5 V minus a fixed 0.1 V**. ST specifies **total bridge saturation drop** of 1.8 V typical and 3.2 V maximum at 1 A; at 2 A it can reach 4.9 V. At 1 A, a 5 V `VS` could therefore yield roughly 3.2 V typically, or 1.8 V at the tabulated maximum drop, across the pump. The actual drop at these pumps' unknown currents must be measured, and it converts to heat in the driver. The table does not predict an exact output at their unknown currents. [ST L298 datasheet, Table 4](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/l298.pdf).
- The chip's 2 A-per-bridge DC and 3 A/100 ms nonrepetitive peak figures are **absolute maxima**, not proof that the user's module, wiring, heatsink, or buck converter can sustain those currents. ST requires suitable external recirculation diodes for inductive motor loads and instructs holding enables low before switching driver supply power. Check that the module implements the diodes and thermal path before powered tests. [ST L298 datasheet, Tables 1–2 and application notes](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/l298.pdf).

## Smallest informative pump test

1. With both pumps disconnected, photograph both sides of the L298N and identify the actual `VS`/motor-supply, `GND`, logic `5V`/`VSS`, jumper and `OUT` terminals. Keep ESP32 outputs disabled (`ENA`/`ENB` low) while supplying and measuring the board. Do not infer a module pinout from the L298 chip pinout.
2. Test **one pump at a time** at the voltage already tried, with a current-limited source appropriate for a short wet test. Record input voltage **at the board**, voltage **directly across the pump while running**, running current, whether it starts reliably, flow through the actual hose, and driver temperature. A DMM's steady reading may miss startup peak current; a current-limited supply or instrumented startup test is needed before relying on automatic operation. Do not dry-run a submersible pump unless its maker permits it.
3. Only if the 5 V motor-rail test proves reliable for **both** pumps within measured current/thermal limits should the two-channel L298N arrangement be considered a prototype option. It will still dissipate more power than a suitable logic-level MOSFET switch for one-direction pumps. If voltage across either pump is inadequate, use a correctly rated low-loss switch/driver after its exact module pinout is verified. In every case, keep a fuse, emergency stop, and firmware timeout; sensor input tests alone do not authorize output automation.

## Servo: exact connection topology, pending its rating

For a conventional **three-wire hobby servo**, connect its **power lead** (often red) to a regulated supply **within that exact servo's rated range**; connect its **ground lead** (often brown/black) to that supply's ground; connect its **signal lead** (often orange/yellow/white) to ESP32 **GPIO14**, which is the project's reserved servo signal pin. Tie the servo-supply and ESP32 grounds together at the low-voltage ground distribution point. Supply the servo from a suitably current-rated 5 V branch **if its label permits 5 V**; do not power it from the ESP32 `3V3` pin or feed it 12 V. Avoid motor/servo return current through the ESP32 board. [Adafruit servo wiring/power guide](https://learn.adafruit.com/using-servos-with-circuitpython/hardware); [Adafruit independent servo-supply guidance](https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-16-channel-servo-driver-with-raspberry-pi/hooking-it-up).

The ESP32 GPIO supplies only **position-control pulses**, not servo motor current. Many ordinary 4.8–6 V servos recognize a 3.3 V pulse; Adafruit demonstrates this with a Raspberry Pi, but another Adafruit servo-tester design uses a level shifter for servos that do not. Therefore 3.3 V signal compatibility of this **unidentified** servo remains a bench check, not a guarantee. Keep any signal presented to ESP32 input pins at or below its 3.3 V domain; if a level shifter is needed for the **servo-bound** signal, identify a proper 3.3→5 V circuit rather than feeding 5 V back into the ESP32. [Adafruit 3.3 V servo example](https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruits-raspberry-pi-lesson-8-using-a-servo-motor/hardware); [Adafruit level-shifted example](https://learn.adafruit.com/circuitpython-servo-tester/circuitry); [Espressif ESP32 GPIO FAQ](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-faq/en/latest/esp-faq-en-master.pdf).

The XY-MOS is a **load switch**, not a device that generates servo position pulses or regulates the servo voltage. It is **not in the basic servo connection**. Do not low-side-switch a servo's ground with an unverified XY-MOS while leaving its ESP32 signal connected: that can leave an unintended power path through the signal. A MOS board may instead be considered for a one-direction pump after its actual power/trigger terminals and ratings are identified. This is circuit-level engineering inference from the three-wire servo arrangement, not a verified schematic of the user's XY-MOS.

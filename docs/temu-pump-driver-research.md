## Engineering implications from component-maker documentation

1. The [ST L298 datasheet](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/l298.pdf), electrical-characteristics table, specifies a **total output saturation drop of 1.8 V typical / 3.2 V maximum at 1 A**, and **up to 4.9 V at 2 A**, before counting cable and supply losses. It has two full bridges and a 2 A DC-operation absolute maximum per bridge in the specified package. A 3–5 V pump on a 5 V rail may therefore receive too little voltage to start or run properly. The L298N is **not a defensible default driver for the small 3–5 V pump**. Test its included MOS controller only after its terminals/control rating are identified, or use a documented suitable logic-level MOSFET driver. The L298N may be used for a higher-voltage pump only after that specific pump's startup current and voltage are measured and checked against the actual module's thermal capability.
2. A hobby servo is a **three-wire position-controlled device**, not a two-wire motor to dim with a load-switch board. [Adafruit's servo-power guidance](https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-16-channel-servo-driver-with-raspberry-pi/hooking-it-up) separates logic supply from the motor supply (typically 5 V) and warns that servo motion can cause supply dips. [Adafruit's servo hardware guide](https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruits-raspberry-pi-lesson-8-using-a-servo-motor/hardware) describes conventional servos as taking roughly 4.8–6 V power while accepting a 3.3 V pulse in its example; [another Adafruit design](https://learn.adafruit.com/circuitpython-servo-tester/circuitry) uses a level shifter because some servos may not accept 3.3 V high. **The user's particular servo must be checked.** It needs a suitably current-rated regulated supply near its own rated voltage and a position-control pulse from the ESP32 (with common ground), not XY-MOS output PWM as its position signal. Do not power it from the ESP32 3V3 pin. A MOSFET module could at most be an *optional* servo supply switch after verifying its circuit and safe power sequencing; it does not replace the servo signal.
3. The XY-MOS may be more useful for **a pump** than a servo if the user's board is indeed the common low-side switch and its verified ratings cover that pump. For the 3–5 V pump, the kit's included MOS board is the first candidate, but no unverified MOS board should be connected until its terminals and trigger limits are identified. The black pump cannot be assigned to the 12 V rail or the L298N from the information currently available.
4. Do not run the 3–5 V pump from 12 V; do not connect a 12 V motor rail to any 5 V servo supply. The 5 V buck needs sufficient current for **logic plus servo plus small pump if they share it**, including startup peaks, or the loads need separately rated 5 V branches. This is a power-budget requirement, not proof that the present buck is adequate. Keep a common ground for ESP32 control signals while keeping motor current out of the ESP32 ground path where possible. See [Espressif DevKitC power guidance](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v5.0.5/esp32/hw-reference/esp32/get-started-devkitc.html) and the Adafruit servo-power guidance above.

## Smallest decisive checks before wiring outputs

1. Clear photos of both pumps' bodies and any packaging/labels, the **front and back** of the L298N, XY-MOS, included pump-MOS board, and the servo model label. Photograph all screw-terminal text and jumpers.
2. Measure the regulated 5 V and 12 V rails without loads, then measure the small pump's running and startup current in a **short, current-limited, correctly submerged** standalone test at its selected 3 V or 5 V rating. For the black pump, first establish its rated voltage from the exact listing/label or seller; do not discover it by progressively applying voltage without current limiting.
3. Measure each pump's running rail voltage and current under its real plumbing/nozzle load, one pump at a time. Compare startup peaks and L298N thermal limits before choosing a driver. The [ST datasheet](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/l298.pdf) also says enables should be low before turning the driver supply on/off.
